# Evaluation (Phase 9)

Generated by `python scripts/evaluate.py` from the real program. Do not edit by hand: a test checks that this file matches the script, so every number can be reproduced. Random experiments use a fixed seed. The data is synthetic (`docs/dataset.md`), so the results say how the MODEL behaves, not how real bus markets work.

## Key findings

1. Each profile's ranking follows the raw value its weights favour: `budget_traveller` follows price most (rho +0.93); `business_traveller` follows departure most (rho +0.83); `flexible_tourist` follows flexibility most (rho +0.70); `environment_focused` follows emissions most (rho +0.84). Rho is well below 1 because the ranking combines four criteria.
2. The winner is stable for most single-weight changes: 15 of 18 weights can move by 10 points or more before the winner changes. The most fragile is `departure` for `environment_focused` (margin 5.0 points).
3. With every weight changed randomly by up to 30%, the winner stays the same in at least 98% of the samples for every profile.
4. Only 12 of 47 offers can win under ANY weighting of the four criteria, and three offers win 76% of random weightings: the weights choose between a few contenders, they do not make every offer a candidate.
5. Influence differs from weight: the largest excess is `departure` for `business_traveller` (+9 points), the largest shortfall `flexibility` for `flexible_tourist` (-10 points). The weights are not the same as the real importance.
6. For the budget profile (price weight 55%) the real influence of price ranges from 47% (Cadiz (long trip), max EUR 80) to 60% (Malaga, spec request) depending on the request, so the same weight does not mean the same thing in every search.
7. Changing one scoring parameter at a time (over ranges up to 4x either way) changes the winner in only 2 of 108 cases (2%): departure = 120 (budget_traveller); flexibility = 90 (flexible_tourist). The result does not hinge on any single one of my numbers, but they remain design choices and the report should say so.
8. Night buses score 0 on departure with the default 09:00 preference, against 69 when the traveller prefers 22:00. The best NocheVia offer ranks lower at 09:00 than at 22:00 for 3 of 4 profiles (business_traveller, flexible_tourist, environment_focused); for budget_traveller the penalty does not matter because the price weight outweighs it. The default preferred time, not the model's idea of night buses, creates the disadvantage.
9. A hard budget is a cliff: OF-0723 (EUR 33.50) is rank 2 at budget 50, still included at budget 33.50 (rank 3 there) and completely gone at 33.49. The budget also shifts the winner's score between 76.8 and 86.2 through the relative price scale, although the winner itself does not change.
10. The field of companies narrows slowly: a single company only remains from level 'L5: + budget 35', and the first destinations with no offer at all appear at 'L3: + journey at most 7 h'. At the strictest level 2 of 15 destinations have exactly one company (Seville (NocheVia), Malaga (NocheVia)) and 3 have none: in those cases the hard filter, not the scoring, decides.
11. Market structure (synthetic data, so this reflects how the companies were generated): the leading company per profile is `budget_traveller` -> SolRuta (8/15); `business_traveller` -> VerdeBus (8/15); `flexible_tourist` -> VerdeBus (7/15); `environment_focused` -> VerdeBus (15/15). Every company wins somewhere.
12. Rank reversal is real but rare here: removing one non-winning offer changed the order of the remaining offers in 2 of 46 cases for `business_traveller` (up to 12 pairs swapped), and changed the winner in 0. The score of an offer only has meaning inside one list.
13. Penalising missing data works as intended: if VerdeBus stops reporting emissions, its offers in the environment profile's top 5 drop from 5 to 0 (best rank 1 -> 36). The risk is the other way round: a company with poor data is punished even if the bus is clean.
14. The ranking is identical in repeated runs and for any input order
